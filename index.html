Weekly Reflection +

Week 1

Before starting this course, I mainly thought of digital media as a set of tools that people use to communicate, access information and present visual content. In China, I was more accustomed to using mobile apps than websites. However, after coming to the UK, I found myself relying on websites much more frequently, particularly the University of Leeds Minerva platform. I use it to check course information, download lecture slides and access online reading materials through external links. This made me realise that websites have become an important part of my everyday learning. One of the first things I found interesting about this course was the idea of creating my own portfolio website. I initially imagined it as a personal space that I could decorate and arrange according to my preferences, almost like designing a room with furniture and objects that reflect my personality. The first week’s lectures also encouraged me to reconsider my understanding of technology. Baym (2015) discusses different ways of understanding the relationship between technology and society, including technological determinism and the social construction of technology. I began to understand that digital technologies are not simply neutral tools that exist independently of people. Their development and use are also shaped by social contexts, values and human decisions. This made me think more critically about my own experience of using digital media in China and the UK. Why was I more accustomed to using mobile apps in China, while I now rely on websites much more frequently in the UK? I began to wonder whether differences in digital policies, platform development and broader social environments might help explain these different habits. Rather than seeing these differences as simply a matter of personal preference, I started to consider how the relationship between technology and society can shape people’s everyday media practices.

Week 2: Creating Websites

Learned basic HTML structure and CSS styling rules, tried to write static page code independently, mastered color matching and basic layout logic, and completed the first version of the personal learning page.

Week 3: Web Scraping Practice

Used the Web Scraper tool to collect the programme list data of BBC New & Trending, exported it into CSV format, learned how to convert unstructured web content into structured datasets, and observed that the platform's content focus is more on crime and documentary series.

Week 4: Data & Visualisation

In Week 4, I organised collected Reddit posts into a coded spreadsheet, tagging AI reading issues like fake citations, shallow analysis and length limits with numerical values. The process showed me how keyword choices and selecting high-engagement posts shape the dataset, and made me see the real student pain points behind the neat coded table.
