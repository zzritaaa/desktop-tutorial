Week 4: Data & Visualisation

Data project · Week 4

This week’s workshop on data practices and visualisation pushed me to move beyond surface-level ideas of good data by building my own small dataset on how arts and humanities students encounter AI tools during academic reading. I chose the student AI reading challenge scenario, collected 30 public Reddit posts from education and AI-related communities, and coded them into four categories: fabricated citations, shallow analysis, length limits, and no reported difficulties. Going through the coding and value-labelling process taught me that turning messy qualitative posts into numerical spreadsheet values is never neutral. My choice of keywords, sampling high-engagement threads, and anonymisation rules all shaped what the dataset could see and what it erased. It also made me confront the gap between the objective numbers in my coded table and the lived frustrations in the original posts, like students encountering fake citations or hitting context limits when using AI for long readings. This exercise didn’t produce a perfect dataset, but it let me see firsthand how data is constructed, and how ethical choices in collection and coding shape what we can later visualise and claim about AI in student learning.
